  Findings behind it (useful for the demo story and for Aiman):
  1. **The battery is always "behind" the load.** Net load (demand - solar) is never negative and is below 500 kW in only 0.5 % of train hours (solar peaks at 144 kW, demand averages 1,120 kW). So the battery never causes export and the score is *linear* in the setpoint: the task is pure energy arbitrage on the blended signal `w = 0.7*co2/CO2_base + 0.3*price/COST_base`. **Demand/solar forecasts cannot improve the score** (Aiman: a forecaster is not needed by the optimizer; EDA + the story are where your work pays off).
  2. **24 h of look-ahead is enough.** The battery fills/empties in ~4 h, so a plan re-solved every hour with the known 24 h of price+CO2 equals perfect foresight (100.00 % on 6,768 h of price/CO2; 12 h look-ahead and terminal values add nothing). The 0.15 % we miss on the real data is two meter outages (2026-06-17/18, 2026-08-06: demand and solar read 0 for hours) that the oracle "foresees".
  3. **Details that are worth real points** (`experiments/mpc/sensitivity.py`): reading the forecast lists one hour off (they start at the CURRENT hour in the official env, `list[0] == preu_actual`) = **-2.5 points**; price-only objective = -1.5; no export guard during outages = -0.014; not emptying the battery at the end of the run = -0.010; CO2:price weight ratio off by 25 % = -0.008, by 10 % = -0.001. LP gotcha: in raw score units `w ~ 1e-5`, same order as HiGHS' tolerances -> rescale costs to O(1) or the "optimum" is visibly sub-optimal.
  4. The agent solves the LP by dynamic programming on the marginal value of stored energy (same score as HiGHS to 4 decimals, 3x faster, no scipy); `tests/test_agent.py` checks it against the LP.
